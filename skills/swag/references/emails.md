# Emails

Make the subject, first visible content, hierarchy, and primary action agree. Keep the message easy to scan at a narrow reading width. Preserve the sender's identity and the recipient's context without adding decorative clutter.

Use text for essential information. The meaning and action must survive blocked images, plain text, and the recipient's light or dark appearance. Links should identify their destination or purpose, and images need useful alternatives when they carry meaning.

Design within the intended mail clients' capabilities. Keep reading and action independent of hover, scripts, or animation. Supporting detail belongs in the message's reading order or a clearly described destination, without obscuring the main content.

For an email template, inspect the rendered narrow and wide versions, image-blocked appearance, and text alternative where supported. For a composed message, inspect its actual formatting and links. Distinguish a local preview from client compatibility checks. Preparing a message does not authorize sending it.
